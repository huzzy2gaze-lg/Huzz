00:00 · Bottom line

What to do

  1. Plain reposting stopped working on Oct 2. YouTube now ranks re-uploaded clips lower when the uploader adds nothing of their own. In our 14-day sample, fan channels that mostly repost lost a median 52% of their daily views after Oct 2. Channels that add something or work with permission did better: a gaming clip page that gets permission for every clip lost 20%, a ranking-compilation page lost 2%, and a fan page that adds art and analysis grew.
  2. Shorts ad revenue alone won't pay you. The Shorts pool pays about $0.03–0.10 per 1,000 views. Creator-funded clipping campaigns pay $0.40–$1.50 per 1,000 views, and memberships plus long-form clip compilations add more. Your permission advantage lets you run a licensed, paid-by-the-creator clip channel, not an ad-pool farm.
  3. The deadline that matters is Jan 31, 2027. On Feb 1 the Shorts route into the Partner Program doubles to 1,000 subscribers plus 20M Shorts views in 90 days. Launch now and apply under the current 10M bar.
  4. Start with the money-advice audit format (Caleb Hammer's Financial Audit and similar shows). It was the #1 podcast on YouTube's US chart in September, added about 100K subscribers in 14 days, and signed a Fox / Red Seat ad deal on Sep 16. Its official clips channel hasn't posted in about 10 months, we found only 12 channels clipping it, and finance pays the highest RPM.
  5. Add AI and creator-economy talk as the second lane (the two fastest-growing mid-size niches in September, with high RPM, and Lex Clips has gone quiet). Run GTA 6 as a 6-week sprint around the Nov 19 launch (gaming is the fastest-rising YouTube category this week). Keep one breakout Kick streamer as an option if a deal lands: Odablock became the first Kick streamer to 100K active subs on Sep 24, and only about 64 related channels exist on YouTube.
00:01 · Platform shift

The Oct 2 change, measured

On Oct 1–2, 2026 YouTube said it is updating Shorts recommendations to "further prioritize original content and reduce the reach of content re-uploaded from other creators without adding anything of your own." It also warned that minor technical edits and template-based changes don't count. Monetization rules didn't change: clips, compilations and reactions stay eligible when they add real value. What changed is reach.

To see the effect, we compared each channel's average daily views for Sep 29 – Oct 2 with Oct 6 – 12 (Social Blade, Oct 9 dropped because it looks like a collection glitch on most channels).

How clip pages actually earn

"Monetized" means more than one thing. YouTube doesn't publish anyone's Partner Program status, so we counted a channel as monetized when it showed a Join button (memberships) or had received Super Chat or Super Thanks. 00:04 · Craft

Format patterns across the ten

00:00–00:01Hook. Start on the reaction or punchline, not the setup. Streamer pages cut straight to the loudest moment, and podcast pages open on the most extreme sentence.
TitleName + verb + stakes + 1–2 emoji. "Kai Cenat Calls TVU To Make Sure He's The Only One Streaming! 😭💀". The 😭💀 / 😭✌️ / 🥀 set dominates streamer clips; podcast pages use fewer emoji and more numbers.
CaptionsBold sans, white or yellow fill with a black outline, placed in the lower-middle third, 1–4 words at a time. Some add a top headline in the same style. Captions are the one constant across all ten.
FramingVertical 9:16 crop on the speaker, or stacked facecam over gameplay for streamers. Podcast and movie pages use a blurred fill instead of black bars.
AudioOriginal audio first. Most clips play the source sound with an "original sound" label. Music, when used, is a low bed or a trending sound on edit-style pages, and costs about half the Shorts revenue on that clip.
VoiceoverNone on the reposting pages. Added voice or written commentary is the main thing separating "added value" pages (JOEPEDIA, DN Shorts rankings) from reposts. After Oct 2 it has become the main lever.
Length20–45 seconds for streamer moments; 40–75 seconds for podcast and audit clips that need a setup and a payoff.
Cadence2–6 a day on the active winners (Burnt Peanut: exactly 4 a day; Worthy: 4–6; HecticSherlock: 2–4; Caedrel Clips: 3–9). The 26-a-day template page (MoeJiOne) averages about 1K views a clip.
DescriptionCredit the source and link to them. The pages that held up all state their permission or what they changed ("I get permission from said creators … for EVERY SINGLE CLIP").

00:07 · Execution

Launch plan

Lane A · core

Money-audit clips with the math

  • Source: Financial Audit (pitch Hammer Media; Red Seat now sells the ads), plus 2–3 smaller audit and money shows.
  • Value you add: an on-screen card of the guest's income, debt and "the fix", plus a one-line take.
  • Package as Shorts Series ("Audit #12, parts 1–3").
  • Weekly 10–15-minute long-form "worst audits" compilation for RPM.
Lane B · evergreen

AI and creator-economy talk

  • Source: mid-size AI, tech and creator-business podcasts that already let clippers work (many run campaigns). They answer pitches faster than the giants.
  • Value you add: a "what this means for you" text card, a cut-in screenshot of the tool being discussed, and a numbered takeaway.
  • High RPM on long-form compilations ("10 AI predictions from this month's podcasts").
Lane C · sprint, Oct 20 – Dec 15

GTA 6 streamer reactions

  • Follow HecticSherlock's model: written permission per creator and a clip-submission inbox.
  • Sign 5–10 mid-size streamers before Nov 19.
  • Value you add: "what just happened" captions, map or mission context, and rankings ("5 funniest first deaths").
  • Option: one breakout Kick streamer (Odablock) run Caedrel-Clips style, 3–6 a day, if he signs.

House style for every clip

00:00Open on the payoff line or reaction. Put a 3–6-word top headline in the house colour.
00:01–00:40Word-by-word captions (2–4 words, white with a yellow keyword, black outline) in the lower-middle third. Punch-in zooms on key lines; no stock transitions.
Value layerAt least one element of your own per clip: a context card, a numbers overlay, a 1–2-sentence voice or text take, or a ranking. This is what the Oct 2 ranking change rewards.
AudioSource audio, clean levels, no licensed music by default (keeps 100% of your pool share). Use SFX sparingly.
EndLoop the cut back into the first frame. Pin the full-episode link in a comment and the description, credit the creator, and state that the clip is used with permission.
Cadence3–4 Shorts a day, plus 1 long-form compilation a week. Stay under about 6 a day: across 18,000 channels, posting above a niche's usual rate almost always cost subscribers (AIR Media-Tech).

Permission checklist

  1. Get it in writing (email is fine): which shows or streams, which platforms, how long, whether music in the source is cleared, and the revenue split or fee.
  2. Ask the creator's team to add your channel to their Content ID allow list, so automatic claims don't take your revenue or block your uploads.
  3. Ask whether they run a paid clipping campaign (Whop Content Rewards, Vyro, their own roster). If so, join it: you get permission and per-view pay together.
  4. If any clip is paid, put the disclosure on screen in the first seconds (FTC endorsement rules).
  5. Keep a permissions log per creator and link it from your channel's About page. The channels that held up after Oct 2 all say this publicly.

00:08 · Method

Method and limits

  • Your own Shorts feed: this research ran in a cloud browser that can't sign into your YouTube account, so the "scroll" used a logged-out US Shorts feed plus YouTube channel search, which shows what YouTube serves a new viewer. Your personal feed is tuned to your history and may surface different pages.
  • Monetization: YouTube doesn't publish partner status. A Join button (seen in the browser) or Super Chat / Super Thanks revenue (Playboard) proves at least the fan-funding tier; it doesn't prove ad revenue. Each card notes which evidence we have.
  • Audio: the browser agent can read the on-screen sound label but can't listen, so voiceover and music calls come from the sound label, the description and on-screen text. Treat them as strong inferences.
  • Numbers: subscriber and view counts come from Social Blade and Playboard (fetched Oct 12, 2026). Social Blade's "estimated earnings" use a generic RPM range and aren't used here.
  • Rights: this isn't legal advice. Permission from the creator covers their footage, but not necessarily guests' likeness or licensed music inside it. Get that confirmed in the written permission.
